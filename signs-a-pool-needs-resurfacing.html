Signs an In-Ground Pool Needs Resurfacing vs Cleaning

Not every discolored pool needs an expensive replaster, yet repeated acid washing on an already thin plaster coat eats away the last layer protecting structural concrete.

Observed symptomWhat it commonly indicatesTypical next step
Rough white sandpaper crust along tiles or wallsCalcium scaling from high pH / high calcium saturationBalance water chemistry; evaluate careful descaling if plaster is still thick
Hollow-sounding patches, flaking sheets, or popping bubblesDelamination (plaster separating from the gunite substrate)Full prep and resurfacing; spot patches rarely hold around widespread bond loss
Rust spots bleeding from deep inside the floorWater reaching internal rebar steel beneath thin plasterExcavate corroded steel, patch structurally, then resurface
Chalky cloud when brushed and painfully rough stepsAged, etched plaster nearing end of service lifePlan full resurfacing rather than aggressive acid washing
