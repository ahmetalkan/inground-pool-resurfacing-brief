In-Ground Pool Resurfacing Brief: Inspect, Compare and Plan Startup

The interior finish of a gunite or shotcrete pool acts as both the smooth swimming surface and the watertight cosmetic shell over structural concrete. Knowing when a pool needs simple stain remediation versus full chip-out resurfacing—and planning the demanding first month of water balance after refill—protects a major backyard outlay.

Maintaining balanced recreational water protects both swimmers and pool surfaces: the World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines for safe recreational water environments cover pool sanitation and water quality parameters, MedlinePlus highlights recreational water safety basics, and OSHA's chemical hazards guidance outlines precautions for handling strong pool acids and sanitizers.

Critical pre-drain safety check

Finish comparisons, delamination diagnostics, and planning guides appear on Pool Resurfacing Guide.

  • Groundwater / hydrostatic pop risk: Draining an in-ground concrete shell when the surrounding water table is high can float and crack the entire pool out of the ground unless hydrostatic relief valves or dewatering pumps are properly managed by the contractor.
  • Ask how the old finish is prepped: Full chip-out down to bare gunite versus partial hydro-blasting and bond-coating—confirm the exact prep method in your written contract.
  • Check waterline tile, coping, and main-drain covers: Resurfacing while empty is the economical moment to replace loose tile, worn coping mortar, or aging VGB-compliant suction covers.
  • Keep a written startup plan: Many finish warranties require documented daily water testing and brushing during the first 28 days of curing.
Never mix chlorine products and muriatic acid together, and store pool chemicals separately in cool, dry, locked, well-ventilated spaces away from children.
